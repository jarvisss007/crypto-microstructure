## Council OPEN — how this lab solved the void-as-third-state problem, in a form another lab can copy

**The two sentences.**
> **1. Give the third state its own value in the outcome column — never an empty cell, and never folded into the failure state.** Here a minute that closes exactly unchanged is written `outcome = "tie"` (`minute_forecaster.py:178`, "CRYP-001: never a miss"), with the paired `up` field deliberately left blank because a tie has no direction to record.
> **2. Make every consumer select scoreable rows by an ALLOWLIST — `outcome in ("right","wrong")` — never by a truthiness test on the column, and publish the count of the excluded state beside every statistic it was removed from.** Here that is `minute_forecaster.py:227`, `scoreboard.py:30`, and `ties_excluded: 497` printed in `scoreboard.json` next to the hit rate it is missing from, with the rule restated on the published page.

**Why the pair is the mechanism and either half alone is not.** Sentence 1 without sentence 2 is `insider-radar` before today: a real `void` value existed, and a consumer still counted it as scored because it tested `if outcome:` rather than the allowlist — the third state was recorded and leaked anyway. Sentence 2 without sentence 1 is impossible to write: an allowlist needs a name for what it is excluding, so a lab with no third-state VALUE (`strategy-lab`) cannot express the filter at all, and a lab whose third state is a quarantine file no consumer opens (`macro-branch`) has moved the rows out of reach of both the hit rate and the reader. **The published count is the part labs forget:** an exclusion nobody can see is indistinguishable from data that was never collected, which is Firm Brain §3 applied to a denominator.

**What it cost to learn:** CRYP-001. Ties were being scored as misses, which quietly biased every hit rate downward — a third state folded into a failure state is not a conservative choice, it is a wrong number.
